Where does Portugal's public procurement money go, and how is it bought?

A spend-analytics view of every contract published on Portal BASE from 2019 to 24 October 2026: who buys, what they buy, through which procedure, how many bidders took part, how contract values sit around the legal limits for direct award and prior consultation, how concentrated the supplier base is, and how prices compare with the base price.

This sentence is computed from the data and rewrites itself when you change the years, the contract type or the buyer. Click a supplier in the ranking to open its card.

Finding 1Health and municipalities account for half of the value.

Of €120.5 billion, health bodies signed €31.3 billion (26.0%) and municipalities €30.1 billion (25.0%). Construction work is the largest category at €35.8 billion (29.7%), then medical and pharmaceutical products at €22.8 billion (18.9%).

Finding 2Half the contracts are direct awards, but only a sixth of the value.

Direct awards are 49.8% of contracts and 16.6% of value. Open tenders are 13.7% of contracts and 50.7% of value. Where bidders are listed, 43.4% of prior consultations and 10.2% of open tenders received a single bid.

Finding 3Contract values cluster just below the legal limits.

50,519 goods and services contracts sit between €19,000 and €19,999, against 12,290 between €20,000 and €20,999. Just below the €75,000 limit there are 11,401, against 1,782 just above.

Finding 4Concentration depends on the category.

The ten largest named suppliers hold 9.7% of the value. Construction work is fragmented (HHI 59), while energy and fuel (top five 64.6%, HHI 1,072) and postal and telecom services (HHI 1,634) are concentrated.

Finding 5Open tenders come in about 11% under the base price.

In single-contract open tenders, contract value is 10.9% below the base price. 84.6% of direct awards are signed at exactly the base price. December carries 10.2% of the year's value, against 8.3% for an average month.

How it is built

  • Source: Contratos Públicos – Portal BASE – IMPIC, yearly files 2019–2026 from dados.gov.pt (open data, dataset 66d72d488ca4b7cb2de28712), downloaded 25 October 2026. 1,555,252 rows; 4,566 contract ids appear twice or more inside the same file and are kept once, leaving 1,550,674 contracts.
  • Values are the contract price (precoContratual), in euros without VAT. 72 negative prices are left out of the sums. Framework agreements themselves usually carry a price of zero; the call-offs under them carry the value.
  • dbt models on DuckDB build a star schema: contracting entity, supplier, CPV division, procedure, region and a daily calendar around one contract fact, plus a contract-to-supplier bridge that splits multi-awardee contracts equally. Tests check keys, relationships, accepted values, reconciliation of contract counts to the raw files, and that no individual's name reaches a supplier table.
  • Entity types come from ordered name rules (for example Município → Municipality, Freguesia → Parish, hospital or saúde → Health, NIF prefix 6 → Central government). The rules are in seeds/entity_type_rules.csv.
  • Privacy: awardees without a NIF, or with an individual, sole-trader or estate NIF, are reported only as Individual (name withheld). Awardees without a Portuguese NIF whose name looks like a company's (mostly foreign firms) are reported as No Portuguese NIF (name withheld).
  • Caveats: bidder lists are missing for most direct awards; the SME flag is filled only from 2024 and inconsistently; base prices of multi-lot procedures cover all lots, so only single-contract procedures are compared, and ratios outside 0.2–5 are treated as entry errors.
